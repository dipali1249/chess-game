Learn chess

A calm, quick reference to the rules this board enforces, and how to get the most out of the trainer.

How the pieces move

  • Pawn — one square forward, or two on its first move; captures diagonally.
  • Knight — an L-shape: two squares one way, then one across. The only piece that jumps.
  • Bishop — any distance diagonally.
  • Rook — any distance along ranks and files.
  • Queen — any distance along ranks, files and diagonals.
  • King — one square in any direction, and may never move into check.

Special moves

  • Castling — the king and a rook move together, once per game, if neither has moved and the king is not in, passing through, or landing on check.
  • En passant — a pawn may capture a pawn that has just advanced two squares, as if it had moved only one.
  • Promotion — a pawn reaching the far rank becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight.

Ending a game

  • Checkmate — the king is attacked and cannot escape; the game is won.
  • Stalemate — no legal move, but the king is not in check; a draw.
  • Draws — threefold repetition, the fifty-move rule, and insufficient material.

Getting the most from the trainer

  • Hints are earned, not given: each puzzle you solve adds one hint credit.
  • Puzzle levels unlock as the game develops — the trainer opens the highest level available.
  • Every puzzle is a real position with a solution line; play the winning moves.
